# Milestone 1: community and candidate distinctions

## Community

**r/labrats**: a public subreddit for people who work in research labs, from
undergrads and techs to grad students, postdocs and PIs. It covers
troubleshooting, career and workplace questions, venting, news and lab humour.

**Why this one:** _(your reason: why you can read this community well)_

What the comparison showed: most r/neuroscience posts are a paper title plus a
pasted abstract, so the text barely varies. r/labrats posts are written in many
different voices and for many different reasons. It's public, and it isn't
built around one show or release, so there's no spoiler problem.

**Reading sample:** `notes/m1_reading_labrats.md`, 40 text posts (Oct 2026).

## Candidate distinctions (yours: write 3–4 after reading)

The brief: *not "good and bad", but something you could point at in the text.
"Some posts cite a number. Most don't."*

Questions that can help while you read (prompts, not answers):
- What is the writer asking the reader to *do*: answer, sympathise, laugh, react?
- What's in the post that you could underline: a reagent, a number, a protocol
  step, a named person or institution, a link?
- Is it about the *science* or about the *people and the job*?
- Could you tell from the **title and first two or three sentences** alone?
  The model only reads about the first 128 tokens of each post (see
  `m2_labels_worksheet.md`).
